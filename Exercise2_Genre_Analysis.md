# Exercise 2 – Genre Analysis and DTD (Recipe)

## Genre Analysis

A recipe is a familiar procedural genre designed to help a reader prepare a specific food or dish. Its situation is practical and instructional: the writer provides organized information that allows the reader to reproduce a desired result. The genre establishes the roles of recipe writer and cook/reader. The writer provides instructions, measurements, and recommendations, while the reader follows and interprets those instructions to make the dish. This relationship is instructional and task-oriented. A recipe generates information about the dish, including its name, description, preparation time, cooking time, serving size, ingredients, and preparation procedures.

The major genre sections of a recipe are recipe information, ingredients, and instructions. Recipe information contains elements such as the title, description, preparation time, cooking time, and servings. The ingredients section contains individual ingredients, each specifying an amount and ingredient name. The instructions section contains ordered steps, with each step describing actions the reader should perform. The rules for assembling the genre require a recipe to present its information, ingredients, and instructions in an organized sequence. Ingredients must be listed before the procedural steps, and instructions should appear in the order in which the actions need to be performed. These predictable structural rules make the recipe easy to read, follow, and reproduce.

## Genre Structure: Recipe

### 1. Recipe Information
- Title
- Description
- Preparation Time
- Cooking Time
- Servings

### 2. Ingredients
- Ingredient List
- Measurements or Amount
- Ingredient Name

### 3. Instructions
- Steps

### 4. Notes (Optional)
- Additional information or recommendations
